Interests

Beyond the terminal

Four pursuits that occupy the hours when code isn't running — and what they have in common with building rigorous software systems.

01 · Physical Discipline

Running

Endurance, pacing, and long feedback loops

Road running is training in delayed feedback. You don't sprint the first kilometer; you find a cadence, monitor your heart rate, and respect the cumulative fatigue.

It teaches the exact patience that model training demands: sitting with slow iterations, knowing that progress isn't measured in seconds, and staying composed when things get uncomfortable.

02 · Narrative Structure

Anime

Narrative density and long-arc storytelling

What draws me to high-concept serialized anime is narrative density: worldbuilding systems with rigid internal rules, long multi-episode arcs, and creative direction where visual composition communicates as much as dialogue.

It exercises an appreciation for how interconnected components resolve over time — a lens that applies directly to designing multi-agent state machines and complex software systems.

03 · Interactive Systems

Video Games

Rules, feedback loops, and emergent behavior

Games are the purest form of simulated systems: explicit constraints, fast feedback loops, and emergent complexity that developers never explicitly scripted.

Studying mechanics design gives you an appreciation for how agents interact within bounded environments — what incentivizes behavior, where exploit loops form, and how simple primitives compose into profound depth.

04 · Scale & Causation

World History

Causation, systemic shifts, and scale

History is the master log of human systems operating under pressure: trade networks, institutional inertia, logistics bottlenecks, and technological tipping points.

Reading history keeps technical work grounded. It reminds you that software systems do not exist in a vacuum — they exist inside institutions and societies that change according to historical friction and economic reality.

Synthesis

The Thread: Systems, stories, and staying curious.

On paper, running, anime, video games, and history have little to do with training transformers or writing Python. In practice, they exercise the exact same instinct: a desire to inspect the rules underneath the surface, understand how parts coordinate, and see a long progression through to its conclusion.
