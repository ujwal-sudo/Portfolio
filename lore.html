The Lore

The longer version.

The About page is the résumé-shaped story. This is the other one — how a wandering set of interests collapsed, slowly and then suddenly, into language models.

Narrative Note

"College handed me a menu of interesting things and a robotics club (RNXG) as the first course. Robots are honest teachers: the wiring either works or it doesn't, and the gap between those two states is exactly your understanding."

01 · Signals

College handed me a menu of interesting things and a robotics club (RNXG) as the first course. Robots are honest teachers: the wiring either works or it doesn't, and the gap between those two states is exactly your understanding.

Organizing RMAGEDDON 2026 came out of that same club — a different kind of system to build, made of 70 organizers and intricate schedules instead of sensors and motors.

02 · Detours

I cycled through a lot of other things on the way: running, gaming, world history, and a long tail of pursuits that each had a season. I don't narrate those as achievements — they were probes. Each one returned a small, useful answer about what holds my attention.

The pattern in the results: I kept gravitating to systems with internal rules you can open up, inspect, and reason about from first principles.

03 · The Pull

AI was the interest that didn't fade. Language models in particular had a quality I hadn't met before: the more I learned, the more there was to learn, and the more it mattered. It's the first subject where my curiosity and my ambition point in the exact same direction.

So I got systematic about it — Python properly, then modern NLP, then the mathematical machinery underneath: tokenization, attention dynamics, training loops. Not just reading. Implementing.

04 · From Frameworks to First Principles

I started like everyone does: frameworks, libraries, APIs. Useful tools, and I still use them every day. But 'it works' is a shallow reward once you've had a taste of knowing why it works.

That's what Pythia-160M is about — building a small language model from scratch so that nothing in the stack stays a black box. The tokenizer, the architecture, the data pipeline, the training loop: every part is an invitation to understand rather than a service to consume.

05 · Now

These days the work is two-track: build useful intelligent systems (agents that analyze code, investigate ledgers, drive browsers), and train models from scratch so the building is grounded in understanding rather than glue.

The lore ends here because the interesting chapters are unwritten. That's not a gap — that's the plan.
